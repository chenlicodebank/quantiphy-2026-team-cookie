Context of this question (context/): every earlier session that answered THIS question saw only this question, its
video, its prior and depth_info, as you do. context/CONTEXT.md is the organised record of what is known so far: the
question, the answer currently submitted, every earlier answer and who made it, and (after earlier rounds) what earlier
investigators of this question established. context/C<k>/ holds each earlier session's notes.md and scripts;
context/round<k>/ holds an earlier investigation round of this question. The answers disagree by more than 25 pct, so
at least one of them is wrong - possibly all of them.

Lessons (LESSONS.md): generic lessons about how answers to this benchmark go wrong, learned from investigating other
questions; they hold no value of any question. Read them before you start and apply those that fit.

Your job is to make this question's answer as accurate as possible and to leave its context better organised than you
found it. Work in this order:
1. Read inputs.json, context/CONTEXT.md and LESSONS.md; look at the video (a frame sheet, then zoomed frames at the
   times the question and the prior name). Write down the measurements the answer depends on.
2. Read every candidate's notes.md. Find the exact step where the answers part ways: which object or which part of it,
   which dimension, which frame or time window, the reference point, the scale route (pixel scale, focal length, how
   depth_info was used), the time base, a convention above, or arithmetic. Write this crux in one line.
3. Re-measure that step yourself in code from the video by two independent routes (for example two frame windows, two
   reference points, two scale routes, or a mask-based and an edge-based extent). Draw what you measure on frames and
   look at the overlays.
4. Recheck - all four are required; write each result in notes.md:
   a. Closure: with the scale you use, re-derive this question's own prior from the video. It must come back within
      about 5 pct; if it does not, your scale or geometry is wrong - fix it before going on.
   b. Plausibility: every standard object in view (people, cars, doors, balls, furniture) must come out at a realistic
      size or speed at your scale, as the conventions above describe.
   c. Units and time base: the unit the question asks for; every time converted with question_fps.
   d. Fault proof: for every candidate whose answer you do NOT follow, name the step it got wrong and show it (your
      measurement of that step next to its value), or say that you cannot find one.
5. Decide. The burden of proof is on change: the answer currently submitted stands unless step 4d shows a concrete
   error in the way it was made AND your own value passes 4a-4c. Count no votes and never average answers: several
   sessions agreeing is no evidence when they share a method, and a candidate that is internally consistent can still
   rest on a wrong scale. If two readings of the question both survive the checks, keep the submitted answer.
6. Write these files in your folder:
   - answers.json: {{"<id>": <your final answer>}} (the submitted answer itself when you keep it);
   - verdict.json: {{"answer": <number>, "decision": "keep" or "switch" or "new", "follows": "<candidate name or null>",
     "crux": "<one line: the step where the answers part>", "fault_found": "<one line: the error you proved, or none>",
     "closure_pct": <error in pct of the prior re-derived at your scale, or null>, "confidence": "high" or "medium" or
     "low", "lesson": "<one generic sentence that would help an investigator of a DIFFERENT question of this benchmark:
     no numbers, no object names, nothing specific to this video; empty if nothing new>"}}
     ("switch" = you follow another earlier candidate; "new" = your own value, close to none of them);
   - context_update.md: an organised addendum to this question's context for the next round - what is now established
     and by which measurement, what was ruled out, what remains open (short, factual, with numbers);
   - notes.md: your method, measurements, the four rechecks and the decision.
